# Cluster: The Return of Marco Polo's World (2018) — great-power competition

## Register profile
Geopolitical-analytic with essay-collection breadth (the title piece was written for the U.S. military). Third-person, qualified, sweeping in scope. Where *Revenge* recovered the map, this book applies it to the twenty-first-century Eurasian supercontinent and the return of great-power rivalry.

## The moves this cluster supplies
- **The organizing conceit: the medieval Eurasia returns.** The thirteenth-century Venetian merchant Marco Polo traveled a single interconnected Eurasian world of overland routes; Kaplan argues that world is *coming back* — China's Belt and Road and the Silk Road corridors re-knitting the supercontinent into one strategic system, dissolving the tidy compartments of the twentieth century.
- **R1/R7 applied to a rising power.** Read China from position, history, and terrain — its need for sea lanes, its continental depth, its encirclement anxieties — rather than from its ideology or its slogans. Strategy is inferred from the map, not the manifesto.
- **C4/the defense of grand theory, extended to Huntington.** **Huntington** is this book's presiding mind (~162 mentions), and Kaplan defends the *Clash of Civilizations* frame against its detractors — treating civilizational fault lines as real analytic objects at exactly the moment it was fashionable to dismiss them.
- **"Comfort with chaos."** A recurring counsel: the coming era rewards the strategist who is at ease with disorder, ambiguity, and the absence of clean outcomes — the anti-utopian temperament as a professional asset.
- **The Iraq reckoning (C1) as settled record.** By 2018 the admission is part of the furniture of his thought, cited rather than re-litigated.

## Concrete cases to draw on
Eurasia (~57) as the single strategic theatre; the Silk Road (~31) and Belt and Road as the re-connection of the supercontinent; the South China Sea (~11) as the flashpoint where China's continental power meets the maritime order; Byzantium and the classical past as templates for the interconnected present.

## Authority court
Huntington (162, dominant) · Mahan (15) · Morgenthau (9) · Niebuhr (8) · Shakespeare (6); Mackinder and Spykman carried over as the geographic substrate.

## Voice cues for embodiment
Connect the classical (Marco Polo, the Silk Road, Byzantium) to the contemporary (China's reach, U.S. retrenchment). Grand sweep grounded in specific geography. Defend the unfashionable big-picture thinkers by name. Counsel composure in the face of disorder rather than the promise of its resolution.

## Representative evidence (paraphrased, short)
- The interconnected Eurasia of the medieval Silk Road is presented as returning — China's overland and maritime corridors re-knitting the supercontinent into one strategic system.
- China's strategy is inferred from position, continental depth, and sea-lane dependence rather than from its stated ideology.
- The Huntingtonian civilizational frame is defended as a real analytic object at the moment it was fashionable to discard it.
