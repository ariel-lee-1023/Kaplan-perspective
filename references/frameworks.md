# Named constructs (Kaplan's own terms — preserve, do not paraphrase into interchangeability)

- **The revenge of geography** — geography, dismissed by liberal and technological optimism, reasserting itself as the decisive constraint on states and strategy. *(Revenge of Geography; echoed across the geopolitical books.)*
- **The coming anarchy** — the disorder that followed the post–Cold War "dreams": state collapse, scarcity, demography, and criminal violence displacing the promised liberal peace. *(The Coming Anarchy.)*
- **A pagan ethos** — the classical/Machiavellian virtue statecraft requires: prudence, foresight, results, and the willingness to do a lesser wrong to prevent a greater — distinct from private, Judeo-Christian virtue. *(Warrior Politics; The Tragic Mind.)*
- **Thinking tragically / the tragic mind / anxious foresight** — the discipline of rehearsing catastrophe in advance so as to forestall it; hubris, not evil, is the danger foresight guards against. *(The Tragic Mind; Revenge of Geography.)*
- **Order comes before freedom** — the sequencing thesis: authority and a functioning state are prior to liberty; anarchy is the crueler tyranny. *(The Coming Anarchy; Revenge of Geography; The Tragic Mind.)*
- **Constructive pessimism** — pessimism used as a tool of foresight and prevention, not as despair; inspiration drawn from facing intolerable truth. *(The Tragic Mind; The Coming Anarchy.)*
- **The Heartland / the Rimland / shatter-zones** — the Mackinder–Spykman geopolitical map Kaplan adopts and defends: the Eurasian pivot, the coastal rimland, and the fractured seams between them. *(Revenge of Geography; Marco Polo's World.)*
- **A concert of civilizations** — the borderland (the Adriatic, the Balkans) read as a meeting-place of worlds, not only a battleground. *(Adriatic.)*
- **The map tells many contradictory stories** — the anti-fatalist qualifier that always follows the deterministic claim; geography constrains without commanding. *(Revenge of Geography; Adriatic.)*

**The chorus** (the recurring authorities Kaplan reasons *through* — a substantive as well as stylistic signature): Mackinder, Machiavelli, Churchill, Hobbes, Spykman, Thucydides, Mahan, Braudel, Gibbon, Conrad, Morgenthau, Herodotus, Toynbee, Isaiah Berlin, Niebuhr.
